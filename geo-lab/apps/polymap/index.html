Polyhedral Map Lab

The globe is centrally projected onto a convex polyhedron, each face is flattened, and the faces are unfolded into a net.

  • Left: globe. Red lines are cuts, dashed lines are folds. Use "Drag: move polyhedron" (or Shift-drag) to slide the polyhedron over the Earth; the net updates live.
  • Right: net. Click any edge (on either view) to toggle it. Cutting a fold splits a piece off, so you can make disjoint pieces like Waterman or Cahill maps. Clicking a cut between two pieces joins them (the smaller one moves). Clicking a cut inside a single piece re-hinges a flap onto that edge.
  • Hovering an edge highlights both places it appears in the net, so you can see what a cut separates.
  • Split face tools subdivide a face flat (it stays in the same plane), adding new edges you can cut along — that's how Fuller avoided slicing through Australia and Asia.
  • Polyhedron: pick a solid or type Conway notation (d dual, a ambo, k kis, t truncate, j join, e expand, b bevel, o ortho, m meta, g gyro, s snub, c chamfer; seeds T C O D I, Pn prism, An antiprism, Yn pyramid). Truncate / chamfer / stretch give irregular, non-uniform solids.
  • Projection within faces: Gnomonic is the true central projection; Arc-linear spaces points by arc length (Fuller-like, seamless on any solid); Equal-area is exact within each face triangle.
  • Minimize land cuts searches rotations for the orientation where cuts cross the least land — Fuller's design criterion.
